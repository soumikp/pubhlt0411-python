Exercise 5 — Accumulate a total

Exercise 5 — Accumulate a total

Worked example. += rebinds a name to its current value plus another.

Exercise 5 — Accumulate a total — your turn

Your turn. Total the deaths and the population across four counties, then compute the combined rate. Note that the combined rate is not the average of the four rates.

# The blank is the augmented assignment operator: +=
# The four counties total 128 deaths across 603,687 people, which is 21.2 per 100,000.
total_deaths = 0 total_population = 0 total_deaths += 60 # Schuylkill total_deaths += 7 # Centre total_deaths += 30 # Franklin total_deaths += 31 # Lebanon total_population += 143786 total_population += 157795 total_population += 157854 total_population += 144252 combined_rate = total_deaths / total_population * 100000 print(f"Deaths: {total_deaths}, population: {total_population}") print(f"Combined rate: {combined_rate:.1f} per 100,000") 
total_deaths = 0
total_population = 0

total_deaths += 60          # Schuylkill
total_deaths += 7           # Centre
total_deaths += 30          # Franklin
total_deaths += 31          # Lebanon

total_population += 143786
total_population += 157795
total_population += 157854
total_population += 144252

print(f"Deaths: {total_deaths}, population: {total_population}")
print(f"Combined rate: {combined_rate:.1f} per 100,000")

Why the combined rate is not the average rate

The four county rates are 41.7, 4.4, 19.0, and 21.5, averaging 21.7. The combined rate is 21.2. Averaging rates weights every county equally; combining counts weights each county by its population. The combined rate is the correct one for a four-county region.

Exercise 8 — Clean a value — your turn

Your turn. County Health Rankings writes "Mc Kean"; the Census writes "McKean". Clean the first into the second, then confirm the two now match.

# First blank: strip — removes the leading spaces and the trailing newline.
# Second blank: == — the equality test, not the assignment operator.
# Order matters: strip first, then replace, or the internal space survives.
from_rankings = " Mc Kean \n" cleaned = from_rankings.strip().replace(" ", "") print(repr(cleaned)) print("Matches the Census spelling:", cleaned == "McKean") 
from_rankings = "  Mc Kean \n"

cleaned = from_rankings.strip().replace(" ", "")

print(repr(cleaned))
print("Matches the Census spelling:", cleaned == "McKean")

This is a real mismatch

“Mc Kean” and “McKean” are the actual spellings used by two of this course’s sources. In Module 4 that single space breaks a join between two real files, and every McKean County value goes missing without any error being raised.

Errors surface one at a time

Python stops at the first fault it cannot parse. Repairing one error and seeing a second is normal, and it does not mean the first repair was wrong.
