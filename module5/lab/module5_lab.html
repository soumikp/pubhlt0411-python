Visualisation and description

The lecture established that adult asthma prevalence barely varies across Pennsylvania counties — 9.7% to 11.6% — while smoking prevalence varies across eleven points, and that the two move together at +0.659.

This lab builds twelve figures from five real files, then repairs three figures that run without error and mislead anyway.

Lab mechanics

  • Each exercise supplies a worked example, then a similar problem to complete.
  • Click into a code block and press Run. Edit the blanks (____) and run again.
  • Hint gives the reasoning. Solution gives the answer.
  • Each exercise takes two slides: the worked example, then the task.
  • Arrow keys navigate between slides.

Three of these exercises contain no error message

Exercises 9, 10, and 11 supply figures that execute cleanly and state something false. A figure that raises no exception has not been checked — it has only been run.

The data

Five real files, all mounted and readable with pd.read_csv().

File Rows Contents
data/pa_asthma_prevalence.csv 67 Asthma, smoking, obesity prevalence — CDC PLACES 2024
data/pa_counties.csv 67 Population, region, metro/nonmetro — the join target
data/pa_maternal_infant.csv 67 Infant mortality, poverty, income — 23 counties suppressed

data/pa_overdose_county_year.csv 402 Overdose deaths and rates, 2019–2024

Three facts that recur throughout

A rate is deaths / population * 100000. A blank is suppression, not zero. Neither region nor metro appears in any outcome file, so every grouped figure below requires a join first.

The course figure palette

Every colour below was measured against white with the WCAG relative luminance formula. A mark that carries data needs 3:1; text needs 4.5:1.

Colour Ratio Use
#003594 royal 10.84 : 1 The primary series
#8a6400 dark gold 5.38 : 1 The second series
#6b7280 gray 4.83 : 1 Annotation and source notes

No two of these separate from each other by 3:1

Royal against dark gold is 2.02:1. Each is readable against the page; they are not reliably distinguishable from one another. Every two-series figure in this lab therefore carries a second channel — a marker shape, a dash pattern, or a direct label.

Exercise 8 — Rates against counts

Exercise 8 — Rates against counts — your turn

Your turn. Compute deaths per 100,000 and rank by the rate. Compare the two lists.

# The blank is population_2023.
#
# The two lists have no county in common. Counts correlate with population

import pandas as pd covid = pd.read_csv("data/pa_covid_monthly.csv") counties = pd.read_csv("data/pa_counties.csv") totals = (covid.groupby("county", as_index=False)["covid_deaths"].sum() .merge(counties[["county", "population_2023"]], on="county")) totals["rate_per_100k"] = totals["covid_deaths"] / totals["population_2023"] * 100000 by_count = totals.nlargest(5, "covid_deaths")["county"].tolist() by_rate = totals.nlargest(5, "rate_per_100k")["county"].tolist() print("by count:", by_count) print("by rate: ", by_rate) print("shared: ", set(by_count) & set(by_rate)) print() print("rate vs population:", round(totals["rate_per_100k"].corr(totals["population_2023"]), 3)) 
import pandas as pd

covid = pd.read_csv("data/pa_covid_monthly.csv")
counties = pd.read_csv("data/pa_counties.csv")

totals = (covid.groupby("county", as_index=False)["covid_deaths"].sum()
               .merge(counties[["county", "population_2023"]], on="county"))

totals["rate_per_100k"] = totals["covid_deaths"] / totals["population_2023"] * 100000

by_count = totals.nlargest(5, "covid_deaths")["county"].tolist()
by_rate  = totals.nlargest(5, "rate_per_100k")["county"].tolist()

print()
print("rate vs population:", round(totals["rate_per_100k"].corr(totals["population_2023"]), 3))

The question to ask of every figure of counts

Does this bar get longer simply because more people live there? If the answer is yes, the figure is a population map wearing a health label.

What this figure earns, and what it does not

Asthma prevalence and smoking prevalence move together across Pennsylvania counties at +0.659. That is co-movement between county averages. It is consistent with smoking causing asthma, with both sharing upstream causes such as income, occupation, and housing, and with combinations of the two. The figure earns the relationship. It does not earn the mechanism.
